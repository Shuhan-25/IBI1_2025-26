Overall, this practical was challenging but very rewarding.

The practical had varying levels of difficulty. The first part (SIR.py) was relatively straightforward because the pseudocode and formulas were clearly provided. 

The second part (SIR_vaccination.py) was also manageable, but I initially made a critical mistake: incorrect indentation caused the simulation to run only for 100% vaccination rate. It took me a while to debug this, but once I realized the issue, I understood how important proper indentation is in Python.

The third part (spatial_SIR.py) was the most difficult. Handling the 8 neighbors of each infected cell, checking grid boundaries, and updating the population simultaneously required careful thinking and structuring. At first, my plots only showed the initial time point (t=0). I later realized that plt.show() was blocking the code execution. After moving it to the end, all four snapshots appeared correctly.

What I enjoyed the most was the plotting part, seeing the numerical results turn into visual graphs and heatmaps gave me a real sense of achievement and satisfaction. I also liked using the viridis colormap; the gradient from dark purple (0% vaccinated) to bright yellow (100% vaccinated) made the plot both beautiful and informative.

I enjoyed the debugging parts less, especially when the code ran without errors but produced wrong results (like only one plot or all vaccination rates showing 100%). However, solving these problems helped me understand the code much deeper.

The most important thing I learned from this practical is the ability to abstract real-world problems into code models. This practical taught me how to break down a complex real-world process into simple, discrete steps that a computer can simulate. I also learned that models don't have to be perfect. The SIR model makes many simplifications (no incubation period, no age structure, well-mixed population), but it still gives useful insights. This taught me that the goal of modelling is not to capture every detail, but to capture the essential dynamics.
